The Constraint

AI ran out of power before it ran out of chips.

  • 01A new generation of GPUs can be bought and installed in months. Connecting a large facility to the grid now takes four to seven years.
  • 02Forecasters see a firm-power shortfall of 35 to 49 gigawatts by 2030, and roughly 56 gigawatts of projects are already leaving the grid to build their own generation.
  • 03On land, cooling and water consume 10 to 40 percent of a data center's power, along with millions of gallons of freshwater every year.
  • 04Every land campus locks billions of dollars to one fixed site, behind interconnection queues, zoning fights, and multi-year waits for transformers and switchgear.

The grid took a century to build. It cannot expand at the speed of AI.

The Platform

We move compute offshore.

Atomarine manufactures standardized floating platforms, the Compute Atolls, in shipyards and moors them about twelve nautical miles out. Each one is a powered, cooled, connected white-space shell. Customers install their own GPUs. We own and operate the long-life infrastructure underneath them.

Power as a service

Customers lease powered, cooled, connected capacity by the kilowatt under ten to fifteen year take-or-pay contracts. We carry the infrastructure risk. They keep the fast-depreciating GPUs.

Manufactured, not constructed

Foundation

Assembled from proven industrial parts.

No single element is first-of-kind. The advance is the integration: shipyard manufacturing, marine cooling, sealed compute halls, and nuclear-ready power, brought together into one repeatable platform.

01

Compute Atolls

Standardized floating hulls of 75 to 100 megawatts each, towable and independently replaceable, so a campus scales in palatable increments rather than one immovable build.

02

AtmoSeal compute halls

Our flagship intellectual property: watertight, dehumidified, oxygen-reduced halls that capture offshore reliability gains while staying fully serviceable for GPU refresh in place.

03

Seawater cooling

A titanium and cupronickel closed loop with no chillers and no cooling towers, for a modeled PUE around 1.15 and almost no freshwater use.

04

Nuclear-ready power

Each power vessel pairs two 75-megawatt reactors for about 150 megawatts, a deliberately small block chosen for redundancy, fine capacity steps, and shipyard repeatability.

05

The Relief Fleet

Power vessels are pooled across the network and reach any campus within about a day, turning nuclear's long maintenance outages into a solved logistics problem.

06

Subsea fiber

A near-shore fiber spur lands sub-millisecond latency at terabit scale, keeping Atomarine in reach of the largest and fastest-growing market, inference.

Power

Gas first. Nuclear at scale.

We start on gas because the market pain is here now, and the platform must earn its credibility before any reactor capital is committed. A near-shore, gas-firmed demonstrator proves the platform, the cooling, the connectivity, the AtmoSeal halls, and the contracts, and it earns revenue years ahead of nuclear.

Nuclear is the scale and margin unlock: firm, carbon-free power, multi-year fuel cycles, and no fuel logistics at the load. That path is now real. The first marine-nuclear class rules arrived in 2024, two-reactor floating platforms have already earned Approvals in Principle, and the United States finalized its risk-informed Part 53 licensing framework in 2026.

The architecture is deliberately power-source-agnostic. We add marine reactors only once they are licensed and costed, into a platform that was earning from gas long before.

Governance

Regulator-first, and honest about the path.

We do not pretend the ocean removes regulation. It replaces thousands of bespoke local land fights with a single maritime and nuclear pathway we can specialize in and map openly. We run two tracks in parallel: classification with ABS, moving from new-technology qualification toward class approval, and nuclear licensing with the NRC, toward a construction permit under the Part 53 framework that became effective in 2026.

Every stage is gated on the one before it. We engage regulators and insurers from the first day, we put no reactor capital at risk before licensing and cost are known, and we prove the entire platform on gas before a single reactor goes to sea. Safety is the precondition for the company, not a feature of it.
